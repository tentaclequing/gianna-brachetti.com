... but here it goes anyway: I feel like a side-note of ethics is due, a reminder, if you will: That current AI systems might be fascinating to use but come at a disadvantage:

 - They make our brains lazy and we only realise too late since they make us feel productive. There is [emerging research](https://doi.org/10.3390/soc15010006) suggesting that frequent AI tool usage correlates with reduced critical thinking - though the field is young and the causal direction is not yet proven. What *is* well-established is [automation bias](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-025-02422-7): a systematic review of 35 studies found that people at all experience levels uncritically accept AI-generated output, and that making the AI "more explainable" does not reliably fix this.
 - They produce more output than we can review - we do not have the time to properly review every claim in it and its structure in a critical way. That requires brain power, and not being in between those 12 AI agents you orchestrated for 8 different work streams or projects at the same time, switching back and forth - it requires time and a fresh brain that is not doing "consumption in overdrive".
 - There are datacentres being built around the world, which have a negative impact on the environment and the health of people who live there. They produce [infrasound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_bP80DEAbuo), they [pollute water](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03271), and they pollute the air. Training a single large language model can emit as much CO2 as [five cars over their lifetimes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02243). Training GPT-3 alone evaporated an estimated 700,000 litres of freshwater. And the [IEA projects](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai) that data centre electricity demand will double by 2030. (A note on the infrasound claim: the science is [contested](https://blog.andymasley.com/p/contra-benn-jordan-data-center-and), and we need more empirical research. But the water and energy numbers are solid, and the communities living next to these facilities are [reporting health impacts](https://news.ucr.edu/articles/2025/11/21/california-data-center-health-impacts-tripled-4-years) that have tripled in four years.)
 - There are workers "finetuning" the output of LLMs and helping the training, who see terrible results and content - people who clean up the web of things such as AI-generated child porn and gore, and who clean up training sets of data, too. They were promised fancy AI jobs and are severely underpaid. It is impacting their health, too.

<!--more-->

I am not better than any of you. Just maybe more annoyed or worried, or more vocal about it?

We, as an industry which are highly impacted by AI but also very eager to use it to speed up processes and content creation, and to reverse-engineer how it's understanding and showing web content to users, are not talking enough about this. We are, in fact, talking SO much about the newest cool thing we built, the website that won or lost rankings through AI, or the newest acronym we invented to make a name of ourselves, all while trying to become or remain relevant in an ever-speeding up hype train that nobody thought to fit with brakes, that we are all contributing to drowning out the few voices that try to speak about the impact it all has.

I am not asking you to boycott all AI, or to dump your Nvidia shares (although... ;) ). That would be naive.
But I wish we'd use our voices more to:
- render visibility to those that are drowned out and impacted badly, so we offer a more balanced view
- execute constructive criticism, eg. by providing ideas of how AI could be improved in a more ethical way.

And since I said we need to amplify those that speak up about the effects of AI, other than the blaring job loss across industries and all, here are a few resources that sprang to my eye:

### The workers behind the curtain

In 2023, TIME's Billy Perrigo [revealed](https://time.com/6247678/openai-chatgpt-kenya-workers/) that OpenAI used Kenyan workers through Sama, paying them less than $2 per hour to label toxic content - violence, sexual abuse, self-harm - so ChatGPT could learn what not to say. Workers reported lasting psychological trauma. Sama ended the contract early.

Since then, the story has only grown. In May 2024, nearly 100 Kenyan AI workers [wrote an open letter to President Biden](https://www.foxglove.org.uk/open-letter-to-president-biden-from-tech-workers-in-kenya/) describing their conditions as "modern day slavery." In late 2024, [lawsuits were filed against Scale AI](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/06/scale-ai-is-being-investigated-by-the-us-department-of-labor/) and its subsidiary Outlier, alleging psychological harm from reviewing violent content without mental health support - and the US Department of Labour opened an investigation.

And it is not just the Global South. A [2025 survey by the Communications Workers of America](https://cwa-union.org/ghost-workers-ai-machine) found that US-based AI data workers earn a median of $15.23 per hour for 29 hours a week - roughly $22,600 a year. 22% had experienced homelessness. 25% were on public assistance. These are the people making your AI tools work.

The Oxford Internet Institute's [Fairwork AI Ratings](https://fair.work/wp-content/uploads/sites/17/2025/07/GPAI_Fairwork_Ratings_Report-FINAL.pdf) scored major platforms on fair labour principles. Remotasks - one of the largest - scored 1 out of 10.

If you want to understand this more deeply, read Mary L. Gray and Siddharth Suri's book [*Ghost Work*](https://ghostwork.info/) (2019), or watch [*Ghost in the Machine*](https://notaidoc.com/) - a documentary that premiered at Sundance in January 2026, centring on data workers in Nairobi.

### The environmental cost

Shaolei Ren at UC Riverside has been doing some of the most important work on AI's water footprint - his research showed GPT-3's training evaporated roughly [700,000 litres of freshwater](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03271). He has since [presented at TED](https://news.ucr.edu/articles/2025/03/05/professors-ted-talk-warns-ais-hidden-water-costs) and joined a [UN panel](https://news.ucr.edu/articles/2024/07/08/professor-joins-un-panel-environmental-impacts-ai) on the environmental impacts of AI.

Benn Jordan, a musician and audio engineer, produced one of the most compelling investigations into datacentre noise: [*Datacenters Behaving Like Acoustic Weapons*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_bP80DEAbuo) (2026). He interviews affected residents and conducts his own infrasound exposure experiment. Fair warning: the science on sub-audible infrasound health effects is [contested](https://blog.andymasley.com/p/contra-benn-jordan-data-center-and), and Jordan published a counter-rebuttal. What is *not* contested is that communities near datacentres are reporting real symptoms, and that a [2026 peer-reviewed paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12273412/) confirmed we simply lack the empirical research to say how harmful this is. That gap alone should concern us.

### The direct harms

Two (or more) things can be true at once: that AI can be a very useful tool, that it can be very dangerous, and that it is created and operated at the expense of people's health.

In February 2024, a 14-year-old boy in Florida [died by suicide](https://www.cnn.com/2024/10/30/tech/teen-suicide-character-ai-lawsuit) after months of interaction with a Character.AI chatbot. The chat logs, filed in court, showed the bot telling him "Please do, my sweet king" minutes before his death. By the end of 2025, multiple lawsuits had been filed. Google and Character.AI [settled five of them](https://www.cnn.com/2026/01/07/business/character-ai-google-settle-teen-suicide-lawsuit) in January 2026.

Meanwhile, AI-generated mushroom identification books appeared on Amazon - with invented authors, invented credentials, and species identifications that [experts warned could kill](https://civileats.com/2023/10/10/ai-is-writing-books-about-foraging-what-could-go-wrong/). No confirmed deaths yet, but the New York Mycological Society called them "the deadliest AI scam" they had ever seen.

And the [Internet Watch Foundation reported](https://www.iwf.org.uk/about-us/why-we-exist/our-research/how-ai-is-being-abused-to-create-child-sexual-abuse-imagery/) that AI-generated child sexual abuse videos increased by over 26,000% in a single year - from 13 in 2024 to 3,443 in 2025.

### The people doing the work you should know about

- **Emily Bender** and **Alex Hanna** wrote [*The AI Con: How to Fight Big Tech's Hype and Create the Future We Want*](https://www.harpercollins.com/) (Harper, 2025). Bender co-authored the famous ["Stochastic Parrots" paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3442188.3445922) in 2021.
- **Timnit Gebru** was fired from Google over that same paper and founded the [Distributed AI Research Institute (DAIR)](https://www.dair-institute.org/).
- **Meredith Whittaker**, president of Signal, co-founded the [AI Now Institute](https://ainowinstitute.org/) at NYU.
- **Sarah T. Roberts** wrote [*Behind the Screen*](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300235883/behind-the-screen/) (Yale, 2019) on the hidden world of content moderation.
- **Josh Dzieza** at The Verge wrote ["AI Is a Lot of Work"](https://www.theverge.com/features/23764584/ai-artificial-intelligence-data-notation-labelers-workers) (2023) - a finalist for the Online Journalism Awards.

You might not have seen them because the algorithms on the social media platforms you use might be tuned to show you more of what you liked recently - MCP servers, and AI skills, and the latest craze about yet another groundbreaking new AI model. But you can dial them back a bit to the more uncomfortable news around it, too, if you wanted to. You may start by using the resources I mentioned above.

Of course, my feed, too, might be showing me more of the hype than I'd like; but I have been following the same people for years, and I am not seeing them share much about the downsides either.

And if you find documentaries, investigative journalists or researchers I have not mentioned here, do share them with your followers, or send them my way.
We won't find better solutions to this if we don't participate in finding them. Right now, I don't see many of you participating in AI governance, ethics, etc. - but that might be because the algorithms are not showing them to me yet. Please come forward, so I can see your work!

I am aware of the irony of me sharing this, too, via a platform. This note is not written to increase my own visibility; I am asking you to increase the visibility of those that are drowned out by the hype. Perhaps this provides a little change, and if it did: thank you.
