... but here it goes anyway: AI systems are fascinating to use, but they come at a cost we are not talking about enough.

There are workers promised fancy AI jobs who are [paid less than $2 an hour](https://time.com/6247678/openai-chatgpt-kenya-workers/) to clean up the worst of the internet so these models can function. There are datacentres producing [infrasound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_bP80DEAbuo), polluting water and air, and consuming electricity that the IEA projects will double by 2030. And there is a growing body of evidence that we uncritically accept whatever AI produces because we simply cannot review the volume of output we generate with it (or our colleagues and clients, who then have us review it all).

In the digital marketing industry, we are all talking SO much about the newest cool thing we built that we are drowning out the few voices trying to speak about the impact it all has. This is about all of us, and how we contribute to this imbalance.

I am not asking you to boycott AI. That would be naive. But I wish we would use our voices more to amplify those that are drowned out - and to participate in finding better solutions.

Three resources to start with:
- [*Ghost in the Machine*](https://notaidoc.com/) - a documentary about AI data workers in Nairobi, premiered at Sundance 2026
- [*Datacenters Behaving Like Acoustic Weapons*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_bP80DEAbuo) - Benn Jordan's investigation into the health impact of datacentres on nearby communities
- [*The AI Con*](https://thecon.ai/)) by Emily Bender and Alex Hanna (Harper, 2025) - on fighting big tech's hype and creating the future we want

If you find researchers or journalists I have not mentioned, send them my way. I might write a longer piece with more resources eventually, and I am trying to make the algorithms on the platforms I use sprinkle in more content outside of the hype for a more balanced view. We should not lose sight of impact, both good and bad.

I am painfully aware of the irony of sharing this via a platform. This is not about my visibility - it is about theirs.
